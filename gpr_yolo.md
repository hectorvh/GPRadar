# YOLO, and how a detection is used

YOLO (You Only Look Once) is a detector that reads an image once and returns boxes. The image is divided into a grid. Each cell predicts a few boxes. A box is a centre, a width, a height, a confidence that an object is there, and a class. Confidence mixes two numbers: the chance that the cell contains an object, and how well the box covers it. Cells that contain nothing are trained to report a low confidence. After the forward pass, overlapping boxes for the same object are reduced to the one with the highest confidence (non-maximum suppression). Training needs images with boxes drawn on them.

On a B-scan the object is the hyperbola. The image is the processed radargram (position across, two-way time down). A box around the hyperbola gives a rough horizontal position and a rough time range. It does not give depth or velocity. Those come from the shape, as in [gpr_basics.md](gpr_basics.md): fit **t(x) = (2/v) · √(d² + (x − x₀)²)** to the samples inside the box. The fit returns x₀, d and v together. `labels.py` turns the known geometry of a simulation into the training boxes. `hyperbola_fit.py` turns a detection back into depth and velocity.

That split is what `labels.py` (a box from the known geometry) and `hyperbola_fit.py` (depth and velocity from a detection) are for. The detector only sees what the processing left in the image. A mean-trace artefact that looks like a horizontal band, or an AGC gain that lifts late noise, becomes part of the picture the network is asked to read. The box is a proposal. The hyperbola fit is the check that the proposal has the shape of a point target.
